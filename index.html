DRG gene-expression responses following spinal cord injury and partial sciatic nerve ligation

Exploratory secondary analysis • Descriptive results • Not peer reviewed

IntroductionMethodsResultsLimitationsReferences

Introduction

Axon regeneration is the regrowth of an injured neuron’s axon. Understanding gene-expression responses associated with injury may help explain how neurons respond to damage.

Following peripheral nerve injury, Schwann cells enter a repair state that supports debris clearance and axonal regrowth (Nocera & Jacob, 2020). Interactions between Schwann cells and macrophages also contribute to the repair response (Li et al., 2025). Differences in inflammatory and supporting-cell responses provide context for comparing peripheral and central nervous system injuries (Mietto et al., 2015).

Dorsal root ganglia (DRG) contain sensory neuron cell bodies whose axons have peripheral and central branches. This project examines ATF3, STAT3, GAP43, and JUN expression. RNA abundance does not directly measure successful regeneration.

Genes examined

ATF3 encodes an injury-responsive transcription factor; STAT3 encodes a signaling protein and transcription factor associated with injury signaling and regeneration initiation. GAP43 encodes a growth-associated protein, while JUN encodes c-Jun, a transcription factor involved in injury-responsive gene regulation. These genes represent regulatory and growth-related processes (Lee & Cho, 2021; Cuevas-Diaz Duran et al., 2023).

Purpose and methods

Research question: How do changes in ATF3, STAT3, GAP43, and JUN expression in DRG samples differ between SCI and pSNL, relative to each dataset’s control group?

This project used processed rat DRG expression data from Supplementary Table S2 of Cuevas-Diaz Duran et al. (2023). Samples labeled Charik and Sun were selected. Charik SCI (n = 6) was compared with naïve controls (n = 4), and Sun pSNL (n = 3) with sham controls (n = 3). The original investigators performed the animal procedures and sequencing; the published analysis supplied processed expression values. This project selected samples and genes, calculated means and descriptive differences, and prepared visualizations.

For each gene, the arithmetic mean of the processed log₂-expression values was calculated for each group. Injury-minus-control changes were calculated from unrounded means. No new significance testing was performed.

Results: Figures 5–8

Figure 5 summarizes group means and expression changes, Figure 6 displays individual samples, Figure 7 compares mean log₂ changes, and Figure 8 summarizes distributions.

All SCI-associated mean changes were close to zero: ATF3 +0.055, STAT3 −0.031, GAP43 −0.047, and JUN −0.113. Injury and naïve samples overlapped substantially.

All four genes had higher means in the pSNL group than in sham controls. ATF3 increased from 8.762 to 11.676 (+2.914 log₂ units); STAT3 increased from 12.656 to 12.995 (+0.339); GAP43 from 12.699 to 13.630 (+0.931); and JUN from 11.410 to 12.337 (+0.928). Individual samples varied, particularly JUN after pSNL.

Meaning and limitations

The selected genes showed larger positive mean changes in the Sun pSNL dataset than in the Charik SCI dataset. This describes the sampled expression response and does not demonstrate greater regeneration.

Limitations include small samples, separate studies, different controls, collection times, procedures, and tissue composition. Both datasets measure DRG tissue rather than a direct comparison of CNS and PNS tissues. RNA abundance does not establish protein abundance or activity, and these figures establish no statistical significance. Figures 6 and 8 visualize the same samples.

Unresolved sample inclusion: Before journal submission, sample identities and inclusion must be reconciled with the source study’s reported exclusion of a Sun pSNL sample. The displayed results retain the project’s three pSNL observations.

Conclusion

ATF3, STAT3, GAP43, and JUN showed positive mean changes following pSNL, whereas SCI-associated changes were small. ATF3 had the largest pSNL-associated increase. Further statistical analysis and direct regeneration measurements are needed to assess biological implications.
